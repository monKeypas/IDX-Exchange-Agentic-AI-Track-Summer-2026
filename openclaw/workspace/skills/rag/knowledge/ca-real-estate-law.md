# California Real Estate Law Summaries and Disclosures

Educational summary for IDX Exchange agents. Not legal advice. Laws change; confirm with a licensed California broker or attorney.

## Transfer Disclosure Statement (TDS)

California Civil Code generally requires sellers of residential 1–4 unit properties to give buyers a Transfer Disclosure Statement (TDS). The TDS covers known material facts about the property: defects, repairs, neighborhood issues, and other conditions that affect value or desirability. Listing agents typically also complete an agent visual inspection portion. Failure to disclose known material defects can create liability.

## Natural Hazard Disclosure (NHD)

California sellers usually must disclose if the property is in mapped hazard zones, including flood, fire, earthquake fault, and wildfire / very high fire hazard severity zones. These often come as a Natural Hazard Disclosure report. Malibu and many hillside / canyon areas frequently have fire-hazard disclosures.

## Earthquake / seismic

Sellers may need to disclose earthquake hazards and, for some homes, complete a Residential Earthquake Hazards Report. Retrofit status (bolting, cripple-wall bracing) can appear in disclosures even if it is not an MLS column.

## Lead-based paint

Federal and California rules require lead-based paint disclosures for housing built before 1978, including a pamphlet and acknowledgment.

## Megan's Law / sex offender database

California requires notice that information about registered sex offenders is available via law enforcement / the Megan’s Law website. Agents typically provide the statutory notice rather than researching occupants.

## Agency disclosure

California requires written disclosure of agency relationships (who represents seller, buyer, or both — dual agency). This is separate from property condition disclosures.

## Escrow and closing funds

Purchase funds are typically deposited into escrow. Close of escrow is when title transfers and CloseDate is recorded. Wire-fraud warnings are standard: buyers should verify wiring instructions by phone using a known number.

## Fair Housing

Federal Fair Housing Act and California Fair Employment and Housing Act prohibit discrimination based on protected classes (race, color, religion, sex, national origin, disability, familial status, and additional California-protected classes such as sexual orientation, gender identity, source of income, and others). MLS remarks (L_Remarks) must not include discriminatory language.

## Licensing

Anyone acting as an agent for compensation in California real estate generally needs a DRE license. IDX Exchange interns answering WhatsApp questions about listings are not a substitute for a licensed agent’s advice.

## What MLS data is not

rets_property and california_sold do not store the full TDS, NHD reports, or HOA documents. L_Remarks is marketing text, not a legal disclosure package. If a user asks “what disclosures are required in California,” summarize this document and state they should review the actual reports in escrow.
